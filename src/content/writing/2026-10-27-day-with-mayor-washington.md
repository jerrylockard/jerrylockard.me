The day I had been waiting for finally got here — Tuesday, October 27th. I arrived at Covington City Hall at 7:45 in the morning, fifteen minutes before my 8:00 shadow day with Mayor Ron Washington, and from the moment he came out to meet me, we hit it off.

From there we went to Holmes High School for the SME PRIME event — the full Holmes story, with Principal Turnick, Superintendent Flucas, and Mr. Shor, is its own post.

After Holmes we stopped at the Myles Murphy Foundation student celebration on Madison Avenue — that one gets its own post too.

Somewhere in the middle of the day, me and the mayor sat down and talked about how you actually get into politics. How he got into it, what his motivation was. He told me his story, and I just find it very awesome.

He was the first Black police officer hired in Florence, worked his way up to Chief Deputy in the Kenton County Sheriff's Office, and retired a colonel — the highest-ranking Black law enforcement officer Northern Kentucky has ever had. After law enforcement he founded NKY Med, one of the biggest medication-assisted treatment centers in Kentucky. Getting that clinic open wasn't easy — the city denied the zoning, and it took years and court rulings before it could open. He served four years on the Board of Commissioners, and then got elected mayor in 2024, the first Black mayor Covington has ever had.

Around 1:30, we sat down with City Manager Sharmili Reddy and Sebastian Torres and talked about the projects going on around the city, including the riverfront revitalization. I've been over there since, and the new townhomes are going up, with ground broken on more.

Then from about 2:45 to 4:00, a code enforcement officer took me on a drive around Covington, out to Ludlow and other places, showing me what's coming. New apartments going up. And Pleasant Street, where the city is putting in prefabricated houses — built in a factory and put together on site — for working families. Two are already up, and fifteen are planned for that street.

The part that stuck with me most from that drive is this: instead of tearing old houses down and putting up brand new buildings like most cities do, Covington is training people to fix the old ones up. Keep the original homes. Bring the old charm back to the city. I think that's an amazing thing they're doing.

That's what I keep coming back to from that day. A mayor who came up through this city, fought for it, and is still building it — and a city that's figuring out how to grow without tearing itself down. I'm grateful I got to see a full day of it up close.
