I got to Covington City Hall about fifteen minutes early this morning. Security let me in and let Mayor Washington know I was there, and he met me at his truck with a good morning. I told him right away how excited I was to be there, and honestly, we hit it off great from the start.

Holmes meant even more to him than a normal stop. Mayor Washington is a Holmes graduate himself, class of '86, and he's stayed close to the school ever since — he's the longtime voice of the Holmes Lady Bulldogs and he mentors students there. So walking into Holmes with him wasn't just a visit. It was him coming home to his own school.

From City Hall we headed to Holmes High School, where there was an event for SME PRIME. SME PRIME is a program that brings real manufacturing and engineering training into high schools — not just textbooks, but actual equipment, hands-on projects, and skills that local employers need. Students can follow pathways like additive manufacturing, machining and fabrication, welding, and mechatronics and robotics, and earn industry-recognized credentials along the way. That can lead to an apprenticeship, trade school, college, or a good job right here at home.

Charles L. Shor was there too, and it's hard to overstate what his giving means for these kids. With support from the Charles L. Shor Foundation, Holmes High School joined the SME PRIME network for the 2025-26 school year, along with Ludlow High School — both close to where his manufacturing story began. That support means students here get advanced tools, teacher training, and real industry experience, with the goal of building careers and pathways, not just jobs. He's breaking so many barriers for students who might not be able to do what they're doing now had it not been for him. His own words for why he gives are simple: he wants to offer people the opportunities he had when he graduated.

I also got to talk with Principal Angela Turnick and Superintendent Cortnei Flucas, and I told them they're doing a great job — because they are. Principal Turnick was named permanent principal after serving as interim since June 2025, and her entire 21-year educational career has been on Holmes' campus. Superintendent Flucas started July 1, 2026, coming from Indianapolis Public Schools, where she led student supports including special education, student services, and family engagement over nearly two decades in education. The kids at Holmes are doing amazing work. They're very smart, and you can see it in what they're building and learning.
